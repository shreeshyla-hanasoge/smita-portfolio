## Gate status — read this first

E9 is gated on **E6 live for ≥4 weeks with a demand signal**. E6 (#8) is not built.
Neither is E3.1 prerendering (#22), which E9.3 depends on.

This record and the prototype alongside it are **design-ahead work**, not a green light.
Nothing here should be merged to the live store until:

1. E6's payment-link shop has run for four weeks, and
2. the numbers below clear the bar.

**The bar, and why the card line clears it earlier than merch would.** E9's justification
is that hand-fulfilling payment-link orders stops scaling. For a ₹1,900 print sold one at
a time, that takes a while. For ₹99 art cards it does not: **every order above a single
card is already a multi-item order**, and a payment link cannot express "any three of
these eleven, and here is which three". The studio would be reading species names out of
free-text notes and re-keying them.

| E6 signal after 4 weeks | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| < 8 orders/month | **Shrink.** Keep the four bundle payment links. Revisit in a quarter. |
| 8–25 orders/month | **Build E9** as specified here. |
| > 25 orders/month | Build E9, and pull the serverless order function forward from Phase 2 to Phase 1. |

Two signals override the order count, and either one on its own is enough: **species
mis-picks** (an order posted with the wrong cards because the note field was ambiguous),
and **buyers asking for a bundle the links don't offer**. Both mean the basket is already
earning its keep.

## Decision

Build the shop **inside the existing React site**, with a client-side basket and a
handoff to **Razorpay's hosted checkout**. Fulfilment is entirely in-studio: art cards
are printed in a run, then packed and posted in one batch a month.

```
studiomintleaf.in (S3 + CloudFront, static)
  ├── catalog        src/store/catalog.js — one entry per species, no CMS
  ├── pricing        src/store/commerce.js — bundle tiers, not per-item prices
  ├── basket         localStorage, card slug + quantity only
  ├── checkout       address capture on-site
  │                    ↓
  └── payment        Razorpay hosted checkout (card · UPI · netbanking · wallets)
                       ↓
                     order confirmation → studio + buyer
                       ↓
                     monthly batch → India Post / Shiprocket → tracking
```

**The product is one line, priced by quantity.** Every card is the same object at the
same ₹99; what changes is how many are in the order (1 / 3 / 6 / 12 tiers). That is why
the catalog carries no prices and the basket carries no line totals — see §"Pricing" in
the [design doc](./store-design.md).

## Why not the alternatives

**Stay on payment links.** Free and already working, but one link sells one item. No
multi-item orders, no shipping arithmetic, no usable GA4 revenue data, and the studio
re-keys every address by hand. It is the right answer only if demand stays under the
bar above — in which case E9 shrinks rather than ships.

**Hosted storefront (Shopify / Instamojo store / Gumroad).** Solves cart, checkout, GST
invoicing and order ops in an afternoon. Rejected for two reasons. The store stops
looking like the portfolio at exactly the moment a buyer is deciding whether to trust
it — and this store's entire pitch is that the person who made the artwork also packs
the tube. Second, Shopify's ₹2,000-odd a month is real money against eight orders, and
migrating off a hosted cart later is harder than migrating onto one.

**Own cart + Razorpay: accepted.** The catalog, the story pages and the SEO stay in the
repo where prerendering (E3.1) and OG images (E3.2) already reach them. Razorpay handles
PCI scope, UPI, and the payment UI — the parts we should never build. The cost is that
we own cart correctness, which the prototype takes seriously (see below).

## The one uncomfortable detail: order creation needs a server

Razorpay Checkout can be opened from a static page with just a public key and an amount.
It works, but the amount comes from the browser, so a tampered cart pays a tampered
price. The correct fix is a server that re-prices the order from the catalog and creates
a Razorpay order id.

Two phases:

**Phase 1 — no server.** Open Razorpay Checkout client-side. The control is procedural,
not technical: **nothing ships until the studio has matched the payment in the Razorpay
dashboard against the order email.** At single-digit daily volume that reconciliation
happens anyway, and a fraudster's upside is one discounted print against a real name and
a real delivery address. Order confirmation email goes out via EmailJS, which the site
already uses for the contact form.

**Phase 2 — one function.** A single Cloudflare Worker (free tier, no AWS wiring) that
re-prices the cart, creates the Razorpay order, and verifies the payment webhook
signature. It also becomes the natural home for confirmation email and for writing
orders to a sheet. Roughly a day's work.

**Move to Phase 2 when any of these is true:** more than 25 orders a month; the first
mismatched amount appears in reconciliation; average order value passes ₹3,000; or
manual reconciliation starts eating more than an hour a week.

## Fulfilment

Everything is made and posted by the studio. There is no print-on-demand partner in the
card line, which removes the whole class of split-parcel problems — and, with it, the
need for E9.2's POD selection at launch.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Product | Art cards, 3.5 × 4 in, premium matte cardstock |
| Printed | In a run, in Bangalore |
| Stock | Not tracked per card — a run covers many orders |
| Shipping | Flat ₹60 anywhere in India, free above ₹500 |
| Dispatch | **One batch a month**, packed and posted together |
| Parcels | One |

**The monthly batch is a design decision, not an apology.** Cards are light and cheap to
post individually and expensive to post individually *well* — the packaging is most of
the cost and most of the waste. Batching lets the studio use one good mailer design and
one post-office trip. The shop therefore names the date rather than hiding behind "ships
in 2–3 weeks": every page that mentions dispatch prints the actual next batch date.

That has one consequence to watch: a buyer who orders on the 2nd waits four weeks. If
complaints appear, the fix is a second monthly batch, not a switch to per-order posting.

## GST position — verify with the accountant before the first invoice

Recorded as an assumption, not as advice.

- **Displayed prices are GST-inclusive.** The number on the product page is the number
  charged. Registering for GST later must not move any displayed price; it changes how
  the invoice splits, not what the buyer pays.
- **The studio is assumed to be below the registration threshold at launch** and so does
  not charge GST separately. The invoice states this.
- **Indicative HSN code** (in `commerce.js`, flagged for confirmation): **4909**, printed
  cards. One product line means one code, which is the simplest this will ever be.
- **Bundle pricing is a quantity discount, not a separate product.** The invoice should
  show the cards and the discounted total, not four invented SKUs called "Trio" and
  "Full dozen" — those are pricing tiers in the UI, nothing more.
- **International is off at launch.** Export invoicing, customs declarations and courier
  economics are a project of their own; the shop says so and offers a direct quote.

Three questions for the accountant: (1) does illustration-plus-print put the studio under
the goods or the services threshold, (2) when the threshold is crossed, does the
GST-inclusive display commitment hold up on the invoice format, (3) is 4909 right for a
card that is sold as art rather than as stationery.

## Migration path from E6

E6 and E9 share a catalog shape by design, so the migration is additive rather than a
rewrite.

1. **E6 ships** `/shop`: the card collection with one payment link per bundle size — four
   links, not one per species, because the bundles are the products at that stage. Buyers
   say which species they want in the payment-link note field. Crude, and it validates the
   whole model for the cost of an afternoon.
2. **E9.3 keeps the same route and the same data file.** `/shop` does not move, so
   Instagram bio links, QR codes on printed cards and old WhatsApp forwards all survive
   the upgrade untouched. This is the main reason the shop lives at `/shop` rather than
   `/store`.
3. **E9.4 turns the basket on.** The payment links come out; `shop_buy_click` is replaced
   by the GA4 ecommerce events, not run alongside them.
4. **Payment links stay alive** for anything sold off-catalog — a commission deposit, an
   original. The basket never has to grow a case for those.

Rollback: if the basket misbehaves after launch, put the four payment links back on the
same page. The catalog, the copy and the URL do not change.

*(The earlier multi-category iteration of this design used `/store`; that path now
redirects to `/shop` so nothing already shared breaks.)*

## Consequences

**Accepted.** Cart correctness is ours. Client-side pricing until Phase 2, mitigated by
reconciliation before dispatch. No order history for buyers — the confirmation email is
the record, and rebuilding it as an account system is not worth it at this volume.

**Gained.** The store looks like the studio. Product pages are real, indexable, shareable
pages carrying the story of the work. Adding a product stays a data-file edit. Real GA4
ecommerce revenue, which is the input to the next tier decision.

**Deferred.** International shipping; discount codes; gift wrapping; buyer accounts.
Each is a data-file field and a checkout line when demand asks for it.
